🎤 Your panel — the official session outline

📄 Download AMG's full panel outline (PDF) — saved to this page so it's on your phone.

Building the Business Case: Leadership, Culture, and Data Foundations for AI in Construction

Moderator: Lucas Hendrich — Chief Technology Officer, Forte Group

Panelists: Jeffrey Picoraro (Chief Estimator, Consigli Construction) · Danielle O'Connell (VP, Digital Portfolio & Enablement, Skanska USA Building) · Luke Reeve (Head of AI Solutions, TwinKnowledge) · Jeff Danley, Burns & McDonnell

⭐ The 1-minute intro — write this before you fly

Every panelist gets one minute for a short, snappy personal intro: who you are, your role, your organization, and the importance of smart technology to your role. AMG says explicitly not to read out biographies — a personal introduction is more natural and the audience actually listens. Have it written down; one minute goes fast.

⚠️ Group briefing — sort the timing early

Meet Mia at the registration desk at the start of the afternoon break. She introduces all the panelists to one another and the group spends ten minutes getting on the same page. Timing tension: your roundtable runs 3:15–4:15 and this panel starts at 4:15, so there's no obvious gap for a ten-minute huddle. Raise it with Mia in the morning.

The six questions they'll put to the panel

  1. Fragmented data sources and inconsistent data quality remain a major challenge — what strategies are proving most effective across projects?
  2. What does it take to build a culture where collecting, sharing and using data is viewed as valuable rather than burdensome?
  3. How do you encourage cross-functional collaboration in such a traditionally siloed industry?
  4. How can leaders be shown the value of data transformation, and how do you make the business case compelling to those focused on cost pressures and short-term delivery?
  5. What criteria should organizations use to decide which data or AI initiatives to prioritise first?
  6. As AI becomes more embedded in project delivery, how should construction firms approach risk absorption — particularly around data quality, liability, and decision-making?

How the session actually runs

Seated panel at a table at the front of the room. Tim (event chair) announces the moderator, Lucas; Lucas introduces each panelist by name, job title and company and welcomes you to the stage individually. Then: "If I could ask you to take your seats at the front, please."

It's audience-led. Mia works the room with a roaming mic and the audience is encouraged to ask questions at any time — the session is explicitly designed to answer their unanswered questions. Take questions between the scripted ones. Interactive format throughout: discussion prompts, not slides.
